# Literature and methods: verified sources (16 November 2026)

1. **Naeeni, H. S., & Sabbaghi, N. (2022).** Sustainable supply chain network design: A case of the glass manufacturer in Asia. *International Journal of Production Economics*, **248**, 108483. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijpe.2022.108483. Source-reported method: multi-objective MIP with strategic/tactical location, capacity and material-flow decisions; Normalized Normal Constraint method for comparing Pareto solutions. **Use for:** integrated decision structure and frontier normalization; we have not implemented the Normalized Normal Constraint algorithm itself.

2. **Mohebalizadehgashti, F., Zolfagharinia, H., & Amin, S. H. (2020).** Designing a green meat supply chain network: A multi-objective approach. *International Journal of Production Economics*, **219**, 312–327. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijpe.2019.07.007. Source-reported method: multi-period MILP with total cost, transport CO₂ and facility utilization, solved by augmented ε-constraint; decision-tree-based uncertainty analysis. **Use for:** our separate ε-constrained MILP reference (ours currently uses a basic ε-sweep rather than full augmented ε-constraint).

3. **Ameknassi, L., Aït-Kadi, D., & Rezg, N. (2016).** Integration of logistics outsourcing decisions in a green supply chain design: A stochastic multi-objective multi-period multi-product programming model. *International Journal of Production Economics*, **182**, 165–184. https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijpe.2016.08.031. Source-reported method: stochastic scenarios for costs, transport, capacity and supply-chain configuration; ε-constraint Pareto analysis. **Use for:** next-stage freight/cost uncertainty sensitivity, rather than claiming deterministic proxies are exact.

4. **Sepehri, A., Tirkolaee, E. B., Simic, V., & Ali, S. S. (2024).** Designing a reliable-sustainable supply chain network: adaptive m-objective ε-constraint method. *Annals of Operations Research*, **359**(1), 921–952. https://doi.org/10.1007/s10479-024-05961-2. Source-reported method: cost minimization, carbon minimization and reliability maximization in supply-chain location/allocation inventory design. **Use for:** argument that OTIF and replenishment feasibility require explicit treatment in a future extension.

The papers support **methodological choices**, not the project's numerical savings or grading rules. The official case document and user's source workbook are the sources for numeric values.
